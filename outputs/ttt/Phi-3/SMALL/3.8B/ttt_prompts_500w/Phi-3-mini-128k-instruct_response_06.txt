The phenomenon is a result of human activities, particularly the burning of fossil fuels like coal, oil, and gasoline, which release carbon dioxide and other greenhouse gases into the atmosphere. The Earth's climate system is sensitive to these gases, and their increased presence leads to a range of impacts that can be both harmful and potentially catastrophic. The term "global warming" is often used interchangeably with "climate change," though the latter encompasses a broader range of changes, including shifts in weather patterns, sea-level rise, and the acidification of the oceans.

The primary cause of global warming is the burning of fossil fuels, which releases carbon dioxide (CO2) and other greenhouse gases into the atmosphere. These gases trap heat, causing the planet to warm up. The Earth's average temperature has risen by about 1.18 degrees Celsius since the late 19th century, with the majority of this warming occurring in the past 40 years. The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) attributes more than 90% of this warming to human activities, mainly the burning of fossil fuels and deforestation. The IPCC's assessments are based on extensive research and scientific evidence, and they have been repeatedly confirmed by numerous studies.

The greenhouse effect is a natural process that warms the Earth's surface. When the Sun's energy reaches the Earth's atmosphere, some of it is absorbed and then radiated back into space as heat, but some is trapped by greenhouse gases. Without these gases, the Earth would be too cold to support life. However, human activities have significantly increased the concentration of these gases, causing more heat to be trapped and raising the planet's average temperature. The primary greenhouse gas is carbon dioxide, but methane, nitrous oxide, and fluorinated gases also contribute to the problem. The burning of fossil fuels, deforestation, and other human activities have increased the concentration of these gases in the atmosphere, leading to an enhanced greenhouse effect.

The consequences of global warming are far-reaching and severe. The most immediate and noticeable impact is the rise in average global temperatures, which leads to melting ice caps and glaciers, causing sea levels to rise and more frequent and severe weather events like hurricanes, droughts, and floods. The warming also affects ecosystems and biodiversity, with many species facing extinction due to changing habitats and altered food chains. The oceans are becoming more acidic due to increased CO2 absorption, threatening marine life and coral reefs. The consequences of global warming are not just environmental but also social and economic, with the poorest and most vulnerable communities often suffering the most.
