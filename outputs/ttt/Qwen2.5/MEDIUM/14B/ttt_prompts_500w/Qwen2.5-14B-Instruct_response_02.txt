In 1995, the United Kingdom was led by Prime Minister John Major, who had been in office since November 1990, succeeding Margaret Thatcher. Major's tenure was marked by significant domestic and international challenges, as well as a few notable accomplishments.

John Major took over a country facing economic uncertainty and political division following the tumultuous years of Margaret Thatcher's leadership. One of his immediate challenges was to manage the aftermath of the divisive and ultimately unsuccessful attempt to keep the UK in the Exchange Rate Mechanism (ERM) of the European Monetary System. The UK's exit from the ERM in September 1992, known as Black Wednesday, was a significant event that undermined confidence in the government and the economy. However, it also provided an opportunity for the UK to adopt a more independent monetary policy, which eventually contributed to economic stability.

Domestically, Major's government faced several internal challenges within the Conservative Party. The party was divided on issues such as Europe, with Major advocating for a more cooperative stance with the European Union, while many in his party favored a more isolationist approach. This division weakened the government's position and contributed to a sense of instability.

One of Major's key accomplishments was the signing of the Good Friday Agreement in 1998, which aimed to resolve the conflict in Northern Ireland. Although the agreement was signed after Major's tenure, his government played a crucial role in the peace process leading up to it. The Northern Ireland peace process was a significant issue during his time in office, and his commitment to it helped to bring about a resolution to one of the UK's most intractable political problems.

Economically, Major's government implemented several measures aimed at reducing the budget deficit and stabilizing the economy. These measures included tax increases and spending cuts, which were necessary but unpopular. The government also introduced the National Minimum Wage in 1998, which was a significant social policy achievement aimed at improving the living standards of low-income workers.

Internationally, Major's government was involved in several important diplomatic initiatives. The UK played a key role in the expansion of the European Union, welcoming new members and promoting closer cooperation. Additionally, Major's government was involved in the negotiation of the Maastricht Treaty, which laid the groundwork for the creation of the Euro and further integration of the EU.

Despite these achievements, Major's government faced significant challenges, including the economic difficulties of the early 1990s and the internal divisions within the Conservative Party. The 1992 general election saw the Conservative Party win a reduced majority, and by 1995, the party's popularity was waning. The party's internal divisions and the economic challenges of the period contributed to Major's eventual defeat in the 1997 general election, which saw Tony Blair and the Labour Party come to power.

In summary, John Major's tenure as Prime Minister from 1990 to 1997 was characterized by significant domestic and international challenges, including economic instability and internal party divisions. Despite these challenges, Major's government achieved some notable successes, particularly in the Northern Ireland peace process and economic stabilization measures.
