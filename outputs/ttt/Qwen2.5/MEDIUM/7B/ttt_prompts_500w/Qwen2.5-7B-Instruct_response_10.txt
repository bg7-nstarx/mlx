The Cessna 172N is a popular single-engine, four-seat, fixed-gear, general aviation aircraft. Its steering and braking systems are designed to be simple and reliable, ensuring safe operation on runways and taxiways. Here’s a detailed breakdown of these components:

### Steering System

The Cessna 172N uses a nosewheel steering system, which is actuated by the pilot using the rudder pedals. The nosewheel is steered by a mechanism that connects the pedals to the steering mechanism.

1. **Nosewheel Steering Mechanism:**
   - The nosewheel is a steerable wheel that is positioned at the front of the aircraft. It is designed to be steered during taxiing and takeoff to ensure precise control of the aircraft's direction.
   - The steering mechanism is typically a simple linkage system that converts the movement of the rudder pedals into steering movement of the nosewheel.

2. **Rudder Pedals:**
   - The pilot uses the rudder pedals to control the steering. When the pilot moves the pedals, it triggers the steering mechanism.
   - The pedals are connected to the steering mechanism through a series of rods and linkages.

3. **Linkage System:**
   - The linkage system consists of rods, bell cranks, and pushrods that transfer the movement from the pedals to the nosewheel steering mechanism.
   - The system is designed to be robust and simple, with minimal moving parts to reduce the risk of failure.

4. **Nosewheel Steering Actuator:**
   - The actuator is a component that converts the mechanical movement of the pedals into steering movement of the nosewheel.
   - It typically consists of a gear mechanism that translates the rotational movement of the pedals into linear movement of the nosewheel steering mechanism.

5. **Nosewheel Lock Mechanism:**
   - To prevent the nosewheel from turning during flight, a lock mechanism is engaged. This mechanism is typically a simple pin or latch that locks the nosewheel in place.
   - The lock mechanism is disengaged by a lever on the pilot's side, allowing the nosewheel to be steered during taxiing and takeoff.

### Braking System

The Cessna 172N uses a conventional wheel brake system, which is actuated by the pilot using the brake pedals.

1. **Brake Pedals:**
   - The pilot uses the brake pedals to apply braking force to the main wheels. The pedals are connected to the brake system through a series of rods and linkages.
   - The brake pedals are typically located on the floor of the cockpit, allowing the pilot to apply braking force with their feet.

2. **Brake Actuator:**
   - The brake actuator is a component that converts the mechanical movement of the brake pedals into hydraulic pressure.
   - It typically consists of a lever or piston that is connected to the brake lines.

3. **Brake Lines and Hoses:**
   - The brake lines and hoses carry the hydraulic fluid from the brake actuator to the brake calipers.
   - The lines are typically made of flexible tubing and are routed through the aircraft to ensure they are protected from damage.

4. **Brake Calipers:**
   - The brake calipers are the components that apply the braking force to the wheels.
   - They are typically mounted on the main landing gear and are actuated by the hydraulic pressure from the brake lines.
   - The calipers press brake pads against the brake rotors, creating friction that slows the aircraft.

5. **Brake Rotors:**
   - The brake rotors are the components that rotate with the wheels and are the surface against which the brake pads apply friction.
   - They are typically made of metal and are designed to dissipate heat efficiently.

6. **Anti-Skid System:**
   - The Cessna 172N may also have an anti-skid system, which is designed to prevent the wheels from locking up during braking.
   - The anti-skid system typically uses sensors to detect when a wheel is about to lock up and modulates the brake pressure to prevent this.

7. **Brake Return Mechanism:**
   - The brake return mechanism is a spring or hydraulic system that returns the brake pedals to their neutral position when the pilot releases them.
   - This mechanism ensures that the brake pressure is released when the pilot is not applying braking force.

### Summary

The steering and braking systems of the Cessna 172N are designed to be simple and reliable, ensuring safe operation on the ground. The nosewheel steering system uses a linkage mechanism to convert pedal movement into steering movement, while the braking system uses hydraulic pressure to apply force to the wheels. Both systems are designed to be robust and easy to maintain, making them suitable for general aviation use.
