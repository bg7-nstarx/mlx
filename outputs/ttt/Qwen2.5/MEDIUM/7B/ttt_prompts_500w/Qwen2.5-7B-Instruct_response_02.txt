In 1995, the UK Prime Minister was John Major, who served as the leader of the Conservative Party and the Prime Minister of the United Kingdom from 1990 to 1997. His tenure was marked by significant political, economic, and social challenges, as well as notable accomplishments.

John Major took office in November 1990, succeeding Margaret Thatcher, who had resigned after a series of political setbacks. Major's early years in office were challenging, as he had to navigate the aftermath of the Falklands War, the end of the Cold War, and the economic recession that had hit the UK in the early 1990s. His government faced significant opposition from both the Labour Party and some of his own Conservative MPs, particularly over issues such as the Maastricht Treaty and the introduction of the European Exchange Rate Mechanism (ERM).

One of Major's key accomplishments was his successful negotiation of the Maastricht Treaty in 1992, which established the European Union and laid the groundwork for the creation of the euro. However, the UK's entry into the ERM in September 1992, under Major's leadership, led to a significant financial crisis known as "Black Wednesday." The UK had to withdraw from the ERM and devalue the pound, which was a major embarrassment for the government and a significant challenge to Major's political standing.

Despite these challenges, Major's government made progress on several fronts. In 1993, the UK joined the European Single Market, which helped to boost trade and investment. The government also introduced the National Lottery in 1994, which became a significant source of revenue for the UK and popular with the public. Additionally, Major's government made efforts to reduce unemployment, although progress was slow and uneven.

Socially, Major's government faced significant challenges, including the ongoing struggle with Northern Ireland's "Troubles." The government worked to implement the Good Friday Agreement in 1998, which aimed to bring an end to the conflict. However, the agreement was not fully implemented until later, and the peace process remained a complex and ongoing issue.

Economically, Major's government continued to grapple with the aftermath of the recession and the challenges of the ERM. The government introduced a series of economic reforms, including the introduction of the Personal Savings Allowance in 1995, which aimed to encourage savings. However, the government also faced criticism for its handling of the economy, particularly in the run-up to the 1997 general election.

In 1995, Major's government was also dealing with the aftermath of the 1992 recession and the challenges of the ERM. The economy was slowly recovering, but growth remained sluggish, and unemployment remained high. The government faced pressure to address these issues and to deliver on its promises to the electorate.

Overall, John Major's tenure as Prime Minister in 1995 was marked by significant challenges, particularly in the areas of economic management and European integration. However, his government also made progress on several fronts, including the implementation of the Maastricht Treaty, the introduction of the National Lottery, and efforts to reduce unemployment. The challenges of the ERM and the ongoing conflict in Northern Ireland were significant, but Major's government worked to address these issues and to lay the groundwork for future progress.
