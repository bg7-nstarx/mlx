In 1995, the UK Prime Minister was John Major. He served as the Prime Minister of the United Kingdom from November 28, 1990, to May 2, 1997.

Background:
John Major was born on March 29, 1943, in East Ham, London. He studied at the University of London and later became a barrister. Major entered politics in 1979, serving as a Member of Parliament (MP) for Huntingdon. He rose through the ranks, becoming the Chancellor of the Exchequer in 1987 and the Leader of the Conservative Party in 1989.

Key Accomplishments:

1. **Maastricht Treaty**: Major played a key role in negotiating the Maastricht Treaty, which created the European Union (EU). The treaty was signed in 1992, and Major's leadership helped to secure its ratification by the UK Parliament.
2. **Black Wednesday**: In 1992, Major's government faced a major financial crisis when the pound sterling was devalued, leading to a period of economic instability known as Black Wednesday. Major's government implemented a series of economic reforms, including the introduction of the National Minimum Wage, to stabilize the economy.
3. **European Union**: Major's government implemented several key reforms to the EU, including the introduction of the single currency (the euro) and the creation of the European Central Bank.
4. **Northern Ireland Peace Process**: Major's government played a key role in the Northern Ireland peace process, which led to the signing of the Good Friday Agreement in 1998.

Challenges:

1. **Black Wednesday**: The financial crisis in 1992 was a major challenge for Major's government, and it was seen as a failure of his economic policies.
2. **Maastricht Treaty**: The treaty was met with opposition from many Britons, who were concerned about the loss of sovereignty and the potential for EU regulations to override UK laws.
3. **Economic Downturn**: The UK economy experienced a downturn in the early 1990s, which was exacerbated by the Black Wednesday crisis. Major's government implemented several economic reforms, but the economy remained sluggish.
4. **Scottish Devolution**: In 1997, the Labour Party won a landslide victory in the general election, and Major's government was forced to negotiate with the Scottish National Party (SNP) on devolution. The negotiations ultimately led to the establishment of the Scottish Parliament in 1999.

Legacy:
John Major's tenure as Prime Minister was marked by significant challenges, but he also achieved several key accomplishments. His leadership during the Maastricht Treaty negotiations and the Northern Ireland peace process helped to secure a lasting peace in Northern Ireland. However, his government's handling of the Black Wednesday crisis and the economic downturn of the early 1990s were widely criticized. Despite this, Major's legacy as a pragmatic and effective leader has endured.
