In 1995, the UK Prime Minister was John Major. He served as the Prime Minister of the United Kingdom from November 28, 1990, to May 2, 1997. Major was a member of the Conservative Party and succeeded Margaret Thatcher, who resigned due to internal party conflicts and her declining popularity.

**Key Accomplishments:**

1. **Maastricht Treaty:** Major played a crucial role in negotiating the Maastricht Treaty, which created the European Union (EU) and laid the groundwork for a single currency, the euro. Although the treaty was ratified in 1993, its implementation continued during his tenure.
2. **Economic Stability:** Major's government implemented policies to stabilize the UK economy, which had been struggling with high inflation and a large budget deficit. He introduced the "Shadow Budget" in 1993, which aimed to reduce the deficit and promote economic growth.
3. **Northern Ireland Peace Process:** Major's government made significant progress in the Northern Ireland peace process, which aimed to bring an end to the decades-long conflict between the UK government and Irish republicans. The Downing Street Declaration, signed in 1993, laid the groundwork for the Good Friday Agreement in 1998.
4. **Human Rights Act:** Major's government introduced the Human Rights Act 1998, which incorporated the European Convention on Human Rights into UK law.

**Challenges:**

1. **European Integration:** Major's support for the Maastricht Treaty and the EU's single currency created tension within the Conservative Party. Some members, like Nigel Lawson and John Redwood, opposed the treaty, leading to a split in the party.
2. **Economic Downturn:** The UK economy experienced a recession in 1990-1991, which lasted for two years. Although the economy recovered, the recession had a lasting impact on the country's economic performance.
3. **Northern Ireland Conflict:** The Northern Ireland peace process faced setbacks, including the IRA's bombing campaign in 1992 and the loyalist paramilitary groups' violence. Major's government struggled to maintain a balance between the different factions.
4. **Internal Party Conflicts:** Major's leadership was challenged by Conservative MPs, particularly after the 1992 general election, which resulted in a hung parliament. The party's internal conflicts weakened Major's position and ultimately led to his resignation in 1997.

**Legacy:**

John Major's tenure as Prime Minister was marked by significant accomplishments, including the Maastricht Treaty and the Northern Ireland peace process. However, his government faced challenges, such as economic instability and internal party conflicts, which ultimately led to his resignation in 1997. Major's legacy is complex, and his policies had both positive and negative impacts on the UK. Despite this, his commitment to European integration and human rights remains an important aspect of his premiership.
