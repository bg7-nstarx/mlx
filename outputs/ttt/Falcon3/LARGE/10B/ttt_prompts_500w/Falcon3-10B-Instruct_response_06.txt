Global warming refers to the long-term rise in Earth's average surface temperature due to human activities, primarily the emission of greenhouse gases such as carbon dioxide (CO2), methane (CH4), and nitrous oxide (N2O). This phenomenon is a significant component of climate change, which encompasses broader alterations in weather patterns and climate systems.

The primary driver of global warming is the burning of fossil fuels like coal, oil, and natural gas, which releases large amounts of CO2 into the atmosphere. Deforestation also plays a role, as trees absorb CO2 during photosynthesis, and their removal reduces the planet's capacity to capture and store carbon. Other factors include industrial processes, agricultural practices (especially livestock farming, which releases methane), and the burning of biomass.

The greenhouse effect is a natural process that helps regulate Earth's temperature. Greenhouse gases in the atmosphere trap heat from the sun, keeping the planet warm enough to support life. However, human activities have significantly increased the concentration of these gases, intensifying the greenhouse effect and causing temperatures to rise.

The consequences of global warming are far-reaching and include:

1. **Rising Sea Levels**: As temperatures increase, polar ice caps and glaciers melt, contributing to rising sea levels. This poses a threat to coastal communities and low-lying islands, potentially leading to displacement and loss of habitat.

2. **Extreme Weather Events**: Global warming can lead to more frequent and severe weather events such as hurricanes, droughts, and heatwaves. These events can devastate ecosystems, agriculture, and infrastructure.

3. **Impact on Biodiversity**: Changes in temperature and precipitation patterns can disrupt ecosystems, forcing species to migrate or adapt. Many species may not be able to keep pace with the rapid changes, leading to extinction.

4. **Agricultural Challenges**: Changes in climate can affect crop yields, making it harder to feed a growing global population. Droughts, floods, and pests can all be exacerbated by global warming.

5. **Health Risks**: Warmer temperatures can facilitate the spread of diseases carried by insects like mosquitoes, which thrive in warmer climates. Heatwaves can also lead to increased mortality, particularly among vulnerable populations.

Efforts to combat global warming include reducing greenhouse gas emissions through the transition to renewable energy sources, improving energy efficiency, and reforestation. International agreements like the Paris Agreement aim to limit global warming to well below 2 degrees Celsius above pre-industrial levels.

Addressing global warming requires a collective global effort, involving governments, businesses, and individuals. It's a challenge that demands immediate and sustained action to protect the planet for future generations.
