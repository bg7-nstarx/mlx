Global warming refers to the long-term rise in Earth's average surface temperature, primarily caused by the increase in greenhouse gas emissions, mainly carbon dioxide (CO2), methane (CH4), and nitrous oxide (N2O). This phenomenon is a significant component of climate change, which has profound and widespread impacts on the environment, ecosystems, and human societies worldwide.

### Causes of Global Warming

The primary driver of global warming is the enhanced greenhouse effect, a natural process that keeps Earth warm enough to sustain life. However, human activities have significantly increased the concentration of greenhouse gases in the atmosphere. The burning of fossil fuels for electricity, heat, and transportation; deforestation; and industrial processes release vast amounts of CO2, CH4, and N2O, trapping more heat than the planet could otherwise dissipate.

### Impacts of Global Warming

1. **Temperature Increase**: The most immediate and visible impact of global warming is the rise in average global temperatures. This increase leads to more frequent and intense heatwaves, which can cause severe health problems, especially among the vulnerable populations.

2. **Sea Level Rise**: As the Earth warms, polar ice caps and glaciers melt, leading to a rise in sea levels. This threatens coastal communities with increased flooding and erosion, and can displace millions of people.

3. **Extreme Weather Events**: Warmer temperatures contribute to more frequent and severe weather events, including hurricanes, droughts, and heavy rainfall, which can devastate agriculture, infrastructure, and ecosystems.

4. **Biodiversity Loss**: Global warming disrupts ecosystems, leading to the decline of many species and the loss of biodiversity. This can have cascading effects on food chains and the services ecosystems provide, such as pollination and water purification.

5. **Ocean Acidification**: As CO2 dissolves in seawater, it forms carbonic acid, which lowers the pH of the ocean. This process, known as ocean acidification, threatens marine life, particularly organisms with calcium carbonate shells or skeletons, such as corals and mollusks.

6. **Threats to Human Health**: Warmer temperatures can lead to the spread of diseases, including heat-related illnesses and vector-borne diseases like malaria and dengue fever.

### Mitigation and Adaptation Strategies

Addressing global warming requires a combination of mitigation and adaptation strategies:

- **Mitigation**: Reducing greenhouse gas emissions through the transition to renewable energy sources, improving energy efficiency, and promoting sustainable land use practices.

- **Adaptation**: Preparing for the impacts of climate change through infrastructure improvements, such as building sea walls to protect against rising seas, and developing drought-resistant crops to cope with changing weather patterns.

### International Efforts

The international community has been working to address global warming through agreements like the Paris Agreement, which aims to limit global warming to well below 2°C above pre-industrial levels, with efforts to limit it to 1.5°C. Countries are setting targets for reducing their greenhouse gas emissions and are collaborating to share technology and resources.

### Conclusion

Global warming poses significant challenges to the environment, economy, and society. It requires urgent and collective action to mitigate its effects and adapt to the changes already underway. Through concerted efforts in reducing emissions, protecting ecosystems, and preparing for the impacts, humanity can strive to limit the worst consequences of global warming and work towards a more sustainable future.
